How to Interpret Weather–Delay Correlations

Understanding the Correlation Measure

Spearman's correlation coefficient (ρ) measures whether months with higher values of a weather factor tend to have higher or lower flight disruption rates.

Positive correlation (ρ > 0)

Higher weather-factor values tend to coincide with higher disruption rates.

Negative correlation (ρ < 0)

Higher weather-factor values tend to coincide with lower disruption rates.

Correlation strength (|ρ|)

Values closer to 1 indicate stronger monotonic associations. Correlation does not establish causation.

Why does this matter? Comparing these associations helps identify which weather factors are most strongly related to flight disruptions, how patterns differ across airports, and how they evolve over time.

Correlations use the selected historical window ending in the displayed month. Weather measurements and flight statistics refer to the displayed month only.
