01

Original Visualization

Understanding the original dashboard, its data, and its intended analytical tasks.

About the visualization

The original visualization is a Tableau dashboard exploring Netflix movies and television shows. It combines several views to describe the catalog across time, genre, age certification, content type, and production country.

Source Original Netflix EDA Dashboard ↗

The dashboard is primarily designed for users who want an overview of Netflix's content catalog and its distribution across different categories. The multiple views allow users to explore both individual titles and broader catalog-level patterns.

The dataset contains Netflix titles together with attributes such as title, content type, release year, age certification, runtime, seasons, country, genre, IMDb score, and IMDb vote count. The original dashboard is designed for a broad audience interested in understanding the composition and evolution of Netflix's catalog. Typical tasks include identifying trends in content production, comparing Movies and TV Shows, examining popular genres and certifications, and exploring geographic differences.

02

Visualization Critique

Strengths and weaknesses of the original design.

Original Visualization and Context

The original visualization is a multi-view dashboard based on a Netflix content dataset. It presents information about titles, release years, content types, genres, ratings, countries, and IMDb-related metadata. Its intended audience is a general audience interested in exploring Netflix's catalog rather than a specialized analytical audience. The dashboard supports several exploratory tasks, including comparing Movies and TV Shows, identifying temporal trends, examining genre distributions, and exploring the geographic composition of the catalog. Its broad coverage makes it useful as an exploratory overview, but it also creates challenges for users who want to answer one specific question efficiently.

Strengths

01
Multiple complementary views

The dashboard combines temporal, categorical, and geographic views. These different encodings allow users to explore several aspects of the dataset without relying on a single visualization.

02
Broad exploratory coverage

The dashboard provides a useful overview of Netflix's catalog. Users can move from title-level information to larger patterns in genre, content type, certification, and geography.

Weaknesses

01
Weak visual hierarchy

The original dashboard contains many heterogeneous views, but does not establish a strong primary analytical task. As a result, users may not immediately know which comparison should receive the most attention.

02
Difficult comparison in the stacked area chart

The stacked area chart shows the number of movies and television shows over time. However, only the bottom category has a common baseline. Comparing the upper series across years therefore requires estimating differences between changing boundaries.

03
Ambiguous genre categories

Many titles contain multiple genres such as "Comedy, Drama, Romance." Treating these combinations as individual categories makes it difficult to determine the prevalence of individual genres. The redesign separates multi-label genres before counting them.

04
Area-based comparisons are imprecise

The original treemap provides a compact overview of content type, but comparing areas is less precise than comparing aligned positions or lengths. The redesign therefore emphasizes line and bar charts for quantitative comparisons.

03

Redesign

An interactive D3.js redesign focused on Netflix content evolution.

The redesign focuses the visualization around a clearer question: How has Netflix's content catalog changed over time? The main temporal comparison uses separate lines for movies and television shows, while supporting views reveal genre, certification, and country patterns.

The redesign makes three major changes. First, it establishes catalog evolution over time as the primary analytical task and places a two-line time series at the center of the visualization. This gives users a shared baseline and makes differences between Movies and TV Shows easier to compare. Second, the genre field is parsed into individual genre labels before aggregation. A title containing multiple genres therefore contributes to each relevant genre, producing a more interpretable ranking. Third, the supporting views use horizontal bars for certifications and production countries. Position along a common scale allows users to compare categories more directly than area-based encodings.

Redesign Decisions

01
Stronger visual hierarchy

The redesign places the time-series comparison first because temporal change provides a more coherent organizing question for the dashboard.

02
Separate lines instead of stacked areas

Movies and TV shows are represented by separate lines so that both categories share the same baseline and can be compared directly across years.

03
Normalize multi-label genres

Genre strings are split into individual categories. A title such as "Comedy, Drama" therefore contributes once to Comedy and once to Drama.

04
Coordinated filtering

The content-type and year controls update all views simultaneously, allowing users to investigate a specific subset without losing the overall structure.

Compared with the original dashboard, the redesign prioritizes analytical clarity over the number of views shown simultaneously. The original is stronger as a broad exploratory dashboard because it exposes many dimensions at once. The redesign instead provides a clearer visual hierarchy and makes temporal comparison the main task. The line chart improves comparison of content types, while the normalized genre and country aggregations reduce ambiguity in categorical comparisons. Interactive filtering also allows users to focus on smaller subsets of the catalog without introducing additional charts.

Improvements and Trade-offs

The redesigned visualization intentionally removes some of the title-level detail and geographic emphasis present in the original. It also remains dependent on the structure and completeness of the underlying dataset. In particular, country and genre fields may contain multiple labels per title, so their counts represent category occurrences rather than mutually exclusive titles. The redesign therefore improves the clarity of the selected analytical tasks, but it is not intended to replace every exploratory function of the original dashboard.
