03 / CRITIQUE

What works.
What gets in the way.

Strengths

Multiple complementary views

The original dashboard combines temporal, geographic, categorical, and rating-based views. This gives users several ways to explore the catalog.

Strong overview of the dataset

The dashboard communicates the breadth of the Netflix catalog and allows users to move between title-level information and aggregate patterns.

Weaknesses

Weak visual hierarchy

Several views receive similar visual prominence, making it difficult to identify the main analytical question.

Stacked area comparison

In the time-series view, only the lower series shares a common baseline. This makes direct comparison between movies and shows less precise.

Ambiguous genre categories

Combinations such as "Comedy, Drama" appear as separate categories. This makes it difficult to interpret which individual genres are most prevalent.

Geographic comparison

The map provides useful spatial context, but exact comparisons between countries are difficult because position and area do not provide a common quantitative scale.

04 / REDESIGN DECISIONS

Four changes,
one clearer story.

01
Establish hierarchy
The time series becomes the dominant view, establishing catalog growth as the primary analytical task. Genre, certification, and country become supporting views.
02
Replace stacked area
Movies and TV shows are shown as independent lines with a shared baseline. This improves direct comparison of trends over time.
03
Normalize genres
Multi-label genre strings are split into their individual genres. A title such as "Comedy, Drama, Romance" contributes once to each of those three categories.
04
Improve comparison
Production countries are ranked using horizontal bars, making quantitative differences easier to compare while preserving the original geographic question.
