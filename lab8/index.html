DKU Bulletin: Semantic Exploration

Interactive visualization of semantic structure and formal bulletin sections

1. Bulletin / Corpus Description

This project analyzes course descriptions from the Duke Kunshan University Bulletin. The corpus contains course-level text covering multiple academic subjects and departments.

Each course description is treated as an individual text passage. The corpus was cleaned and processed to support semantic embedding, topic modeling, and interactive exploration of relationships among courses and bulletin sections.

5. Semantic Findings

These findings use the Semantic Embedding Map, the Topic × Course Subject Matrix, and the underlying course descriptions. A Topic is a cluster of similar descriptions; a Course Subject is the Bulletin's formal category.

What are the major semantic topics?

The model identifies eight topics. Topic 1 is the largest (143 passages) and is concentrated in Mathematics, Computer Science, Physics, and Statistics. Topic 0 (112) contains many Public Policy, Economics, and Environment courses. Topic 6 (109) is especially common in Media and Arts and includes courses about visual and media practices. Topic 3 (54) is strongly associated with Biology and health-related courses. These descriptions are interpretations of the course content; the visualization itself labels the clusters Topic 0–7.

Which topics cross Course Subjects?

Topic 1 appears in Mathematics (32 passages), Computer Science (27), Physics (18), and Statistics (13), showing that quantitative content crosses formal subject boundaries. Topic 3 appears in Biology (31), Environment (7), and Global Health (6). Topic 4 also spans History (28), Literature (19), and Political Science (19).

Which Course Subjects are semantically diverse?

History spans six topics: most of its passages fall in Topics 4 and 2, but others appear in Topics 0, 3, 6, and 7. Cultural Anthropology spans five topics, with substantial groups in Topics 4 and 6. Philosophy has passages in seven of the eight topics, although Topic 7 dominates. Counting occupied topics reveals breadth; the matrix cell counts also show whether that breadth is balanced or driven by just a few courses.

Which passages connect different subjects?

Cell and Molecular Biology (BIOL 201) and Biological Basis of Disease (GLHLTH 305) both fall in Topic 3. Their descriptions connect biological mechanisms with health and disease despite belonging to different Course Subjects. Likewise, Ancient Chinese History and Philosophy (HIST 101/PHIL 102) and From Empire to Nation (GCHINA 201) both fall in Topic 2, linking historical study with China-focused coursework. Shared Topic membership suggests a relationship; it does not mean the descriptions are identical.

Which passages are unusual within their subject?

Pandemics in World History from the Black Death to COVID-19 (HIST 212) is assigned to Topic 3, whereas most History passages are in Topics 4 and 2. Its emphasis on pandemics helps explain why the model places it with biology and health-related descriptions. This is an interdisciplinary course, rather than necessarily a clustering error.

What does searching for “credit” reveal?

Searching the underlying description text finds “credit” in 76 passages across all eight topics. It therefore does not occupy one semantic region: the term is used in descriptions of otherwise different courses. In this course-description corpus, “graduation” has no matches, while “registration” and “academic integrity” each occur in one passage. These results describe the selected corpus, not the entire Bulletin.

6. Design Description

I extracted course descriptions from the Duke Kunshan University Bulletin and treated each description as one passage. The extraction retained metadata such as Course Subject, course code, title, credits, and page number. After cleaning the text, I encoded each passage with the SentenceTransformer model all-MiniLM-L6-v2. This produces a numerical embedding that represents the passage's language and content. I then used UMAP with cosine distance, 15 neighbors, and min_dist = 0.15 to project the embeddings into two dimensions. Nearby points on the map can indicate related descriptions, although the two axes do not have independent substantive meanings.

I applied KMeans to the original embeddings, using eight clusters rather than clustering the two-dimensional map. The current pipeline assigns these clusters the neutral labels Topic 0 through Topic 7; the thematic descriptions in the findings are interpretations based on course examples and subject counts.

In the embedding map, each circle is a course passage. Position represents its UMAP coordinates, color identifies its Topic, and circle size reflects word count. The Topic × Course Subject Matrix places Topics in columns and formal Course Subjects in rows; darker cells contain more passages. Consistent Topic colors connect the two views. Search and dropdown filters help locate passages, while clicking a map point opens its description and highlights the corresponding matrix cell. Clicking a matrix cell highlights its passages on the map. Together, these interactions let readers move between individual courses and the broader distribution of topics.
