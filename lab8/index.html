DKU Bulletin: Semantic Exploration

Interactive visualization of semantic structure and formal bulletin sections

1. Bulletin / Corpus Description

This project analyzes course descriptions from the Duke Kunshan University Bulletin. The corpus contains course-level text covering multiple academic subjects and departments.

Each course description is treated as an individual text passage. The corpus was cleaned and processed to support semantic embedding, topic modeling, and interactive exploration of relationships among courses and bulletin sections.

5. Semantic Findings

Patterns observed in the embedding map and Topic × Course Subject Matrix.

Major semantic topics. The eight clusters cover different kinds of course content. Topic 1 is the largest (143 passages), with many Mathematics, Computer Science, Physics, and Statistics courses. Topic 0 includes many Public Policy, Economics, and Environment courses. Topic 3 concentrates on biology and health, while Topic 6 contains many Media and Arts courses. These are interpretations of the course descriptions; the visualization labels the clusters Topic 0–7.

Topics across Course Subjects. Topic 1 appears in Mathematics (32 passages), Computer Science (27), Physics (18), and Statistics (13). Topic 3 appears in Biology (31), Environment (7), and Global Health (6). The matrix makes these shared topics visible across formal subject categories.

Semantically diverse subjects. History contains passages in six topics, mainly Topics 4 and 2. Cultural Anthropology spans five topics, while Philosophy appears in seven, although most of its passages belong to Topic 7. Thus, a subject can cover several kinds of content even when one topic dominates its courses.

Similar passages from different subjects. Cell and Molecular Biology (BIOL 201) and Biological Basis of Disease (GLHLTH 305) both belong to Topic 3. Their descriptions connect biological mechanisms with health and disease despite having different Course Subjects.

An unusual passage. Pandemics in World History from the Black Death to COVID-19 (HIST 212) is assigned to Topic 3, whereas most History passages belong to Topics 4 and 2. Its focus on pandemics connects it with the map's biology and health-related courses.

Search finding. The word “credit” occurs in 76 description passages distributed across all eight topics, rather than in one semantic region. “Graduation” has no matches in this course-description corpus; “registration” and “academic integrity” each appear in one passage. These searches do not cover other Bulletin sections.

6. Design Description

Data processing, visual encodings, and coordinated interaction.

I extracted course descriptions from the Duke Kunshan University Bulletin and treated each description as one passage. The extraction retained metadata including Course Subject, course code, title, credits, and page number. After cleaning the text, I used the SentenceTransformer model all-MiniLM-L6-v2 to produce an embedding for each passage. UMAP projected these embeddings into two dimensions using cosine distance, 15 neighbors, and min_dist = 0.15. Points close together on the map may have related content, but the axes do not represent named academic dimensions.

I applied KMeans to the original embeddings to generate eight clusters. The current pipeline labels them Topic 0 through Topic 7. The subject patterns and course descriptions help interpret what each cluster contains, but those interpretations are separate from the assigned numeric labels.

Each circle in the embedding map represents one course description. Its position comes from UMAP, its color identifies its Topic, and its size reflects word count. In the Topic × Course Subject Matrix, rows represent formal Course Subjects, columns represent Topics, and darker cells contain more passages. Topic colors remain consistent across both views.

Search and dropdown filters help locate relevant courses. Clicking a map point reveals the passage and highlights its matrix cell; clicking a matrix cell highlights the corresponding passages on the map. These linked actions connect individual descriptions with the overall distribution of topics across Course Subjects.
